Politi-Knows

A game where your class picks a new SK Chairperson.

You will play pretend candidates and voters. Think hard, listen well, and vote for the person who will help your community the most!

A political strategy and civic education card game for 7 to 10 players that simulates the campaign and election of a Sangguniang Kabataan (SK) Chairperson. This is the Playtesting Edition.

What is the goal?

Candidates

Win the election

Show good ideas, make friends, and get the most votes.

Voters

Find the honest leader

Ask questions, find out who is playing fair, and vote wisely.

Moderator

Be the referee

The moderator does not play. They keep secrets and make sure everyone follows the rules.

The game explores how political choices, campaign promises, corruption, and civic participation influence electoral outcomes and governance.

Big idea: voting is how a community chooses its leader, and a good voter checks who is honest first.

Secret roles

Everyone gets one Role Card. Keep it secret! Only show it if a rule tells you to.

Most players

Voter

You play Action Cards to learn about the candidates. Then you vote for one.

Uses Action Cards to investigate candidates and influence the campaign. Votes for one candidate during the election.

1 player

Civic Candidate

The honest candidate. You win by sharing good ideas for the community.

Campaigns by presenting policy proposals and seeks to win through policy commitments and voter support.

1 or 2 players

TraPo Candidate

TraPo means a “traditional politician.” You campaign too, but you can secretly offer rewards for votes. That is cheating, and it is called vote buying.

Campaigns like other candidates, but may secretly initiate vote-buying deals during the campaign.

What you need

Role Cards

10 cards. They give secret jobs.

Platform Cards

45 cards. Each is a plan to help the community. 40 follow the 10 CBYDP topics, and 5 are blank so candidates can write their own idea.

Action Cards

40 cards for voters. They help you learn the truth.

Vote Buying Cards

5 cards. Only the moderator holds them.

Ballot Chips and Ballot Box

Everyone gets 1 chip to vote. Use an erasable marker to write your choice.

Influence Points (IP)

Tokens that show how much trust and power you have.

Set up the game

  1. Pick a moderator.

  2. Shuffle the Role Cards and hand one to each player. Peek privately.

  3. Make two piles: Platform Cards and Action Cards. Put the CBYDP list where everyone can see it.

  4. Get the ballot box, chips, IP tokens, and Vote Buying Cards ready. The moderator keeps the Vote Buying Cards.

  5. Give starting Influence Points: voters get 3 IP, candidates get 10 IP.

Hand limits: a candidate holds up to 3 Platform Cards. A voter holds up to 2 Action Cards.

Phase 1: The campaign

The campaign is the time before election day when candidates try to win trust. In our game it is 3 rounds. Every round has the same 3 steps.

The 10-day campaign period is condensed into three campaign rounds. Each round follows the same sequence.

  1. Morning Briefing: draw cards

    Candidates: draw 5 Platform Cards, keep 3, discard the rest.

    Voters: draw 1 Action Card. You may keep it and use it any time this round.

    If the Action deck runs out, the moderator reshuffles the discard pile. If there is nothing to reshuffle, nobody draws until cards are available. Voters draw a replacement right after playing an Action Card, and unused cards stay in hand between rounds.

  2. Platform and Action Phase: take turns

    Candidates: play 1 Platform Card face up, say which CBYDP topic it is, and explain how it helps the community. It costs you 1 IP.

    Voters: play up to 1 Action Card, or pass. If you play one, you get 1 IP from the bank.

    Played Platform Cards cannot be reused unless the deck runs out and is reshuffled. Used Action Cards go to the discard pile.

  3. Vote Buying Deal: TraPo phase

    The moderator asks everyone except the TraPo candidate(s) to close their eyes. Then the secret deals begin. See the box below.

After step 3, start the next round. Stop after round 3.

How secret vote buying works

A TraPo can offer a voter a reward in exchange for their vote. The voter says yes or no in secret. Only the moderator knows.

Voter says YES

The voter keeps the Vote Buying Card secretly and gets +2 IP from the candidate. The TraPo gets +3 IP from the bank. The moderator writes the deal down.

Voter says NO

The voter gives the card back. The TraPo loses 2 IP, which goes to the bank. No deal is recorded.

Only five Vote Buying Cards exist. The moderator makes sure no more than five are issued and every accepted deal is recorded accurately.

Remember: vote buying is wrong in real life. In this game we learn how it works so we can spot it and stop it.

Action Cards

Voters use these to find out the truth. Tap a card name to read it.

Reveal

4 cards

Pick a candidate. The moderator secretly shows you if they are Civic or TraPo. Keep it a secret!

Privately reveals whether a selected candidate is a Civic Candidate or a TraPo Candidate. The voter must keep the information secret.

Whistleblow

2 cards

Say out loud, “I think this candidate is a TraPo!” That candidate must show their role to everyone.

Right? You gain 3 IP and the TraPo loses 3 IP.
Wrong? You lose 3 IP. (You cannot go below 0.)

Accuses a candidate of being a TraPo Candidate and forces them to reveal their role.

People’s Question

9 cards

Choose a CBYDP topic, like Health. Every candidate must say out loud if they support it. If they have a matching Platform Card, they can show it as proof.

The voter selects one CBYDP category. All candidates must publicly state whether they support it. Candidates with a matching Platform Card may place it face-up to demonstrate their commitment.

Pulong-Pulong (Town Hall Meeting)

9 cards

Every candidate announces their number one topic. For the rest of the round, at least half of their Platform Cards must match that topic.

“Half” rounds up: 1 of 3 is not enough, but 2 of 3 works. 2 of 5 is not enough, but 3 of 5 works.

A candidate may declare a category matching a Platform Card already played this round. The rule applies only to cards played after the declaration.

Policy Peek

8 cards

Pick a candidate and secretly look at 2 of their Platform Cards. Then tell everyone if you support their plan or not.

The voter selects a candidate and privately inspects two of their Platform Cards, then publicly declares whether they endorse the candidate’s platform.

Padrino System

8 cards

Secretly look at the top 3 Action Cards. Keep 1. Put the rest back and shuffle.

If fewer than three cards remain, the moderator reshuffles available discards as necessary.

Phase 2: Election day

  1. Silence Period

    The campaign is over. No more cards or deals. Candidates may give one short final speech asking for support. The moderator decides before the game if speeches are optional or required.

  2. Secret Ballot

    Everyone gets 1 Ballot Chip and writes their choice on their paper, then drops it in the box. Nobody shows their vote.

  3. Count the votes

    The moderator opens the box, counts out loud, and certifies the result with the COMELEC stamp. The candidate with the most votes becomes SK Chairperson-Elect.

What if there is a tie?

1st

Runoff

Two tied candidates give short extra speeches, then everyone votes again. With 3 tied, the two finalists go to the runoff. The moderator uses a fair rule announced before the game.

2nd

More IP wins

Still tied after the runoff? The candidate with more Influence Points wins.

3rd

Coin toss

Same IP too? The moderator flips a coin in front of everyone.

Influence Points (IP)

IP shows how much people trust you and how much power you have. You gain and lose IP while you play.

Influence Points represent political influence, credibility, and the ability to hold public officials accountable. The moderator tracks all changes and resolves disputes.

Quick answers

How many players?

7 to 10, plus one moderator who does not play.

How many campaign rounds?

3 rounds. Each has Morning Briefing, Platform and Action Phase, and the TraPo Vote Buying phase.

Can a voter play many Action Cards in a round?

No. Up to 1 per round.

Can candidates vote for themselves?

Yes. Every player has 1 vote.

What should we talk about when the game ends?

Did voters care most about ideas, influence, or secret deals? Did vote buying change who won? Could voters spot cheating? What does this teach us about real elections?

Elections choose who leads. Good voters also check whether leaders keep their promises.
